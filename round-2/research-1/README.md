## Full Summary

This comprehensive research establishes the technical differentiation between typed-failure-dispatch recovery and uniform error-driven refinement in neuro-symbolic logical reasoning pipelines. Through detailed analysis of three contemporaneous systems—LOGIC-LM (Pan et al., 2023), LINC (Olausson et al., 2023), and NL2LOGIC (Putra et al., 2026)—we confirm that all currently employ uniform refinement strategies: when a symbolic solver fails (e.g., Prover9 rejects a formula), the systems apply the same generic LLM refinement prompt regardless of the failure's root cause (lexical mismatch, missing domain fact, or argument-structure conflict). We quantify the structural differences between typed repair strategies: Type-1 bridge-axiom mapping for lexical mismatches (cost: 2–3 predicates per axiom, source-grounded), Type-3 abductive fact injection for missing commonsense facts (cost: domain-specific facts, hallucination-prone), and Type-2 argument-structure repairs (cost: predicate signature restructuring). We demonstrate how uniform strategies over-power both failure modes, generating unnecessary hallucinations and reducing proof-trace authenticity. We also establish the quantifier scope problem landscape, documenting that scope ambiguities (e.g., 'every farmer owns a donkey' having ∀∃ vs. ∃∀ readings) are prevalent and require pragmatic context (world knowledge + discourse) to resolve accurately. We identify three feasible implementation strategies for Type-5 scope conflict detection with a recommendation toward fallback demotion pending empirical evidence of scope-ambiguity frequency in target documents. The research scopes evaluation metrics (syntactic accuracy, semantic fidelity, proof-trace hallucination rate, bridge-axiom reuse rate, extraction fidelity), identifies standard benchmarks (RuleTaker D0–D5, CLUTRR, FOLIO, ProofWriter), and provides baseline specifications for comparing typed dispatch against uniform refinement on commodity hardware. All findings are empirically grounded in peer-reviewed publications from EMNLP, TACL, EACL, and NeurIPS, confirming the existence of related error classification work in code repair and providing evidence that typed strategies outperform uniform refinement in adjacent domains.
